Fifth-year CS student here. Things I wish someone had told me before Day 1.

**Academics at NEU — What's Different**
The semester system at Northeastern is compressed. Courses move fast. Do not fall behind in the first two weeks — catching up is genuinely difficult once the pace sets in. The quarter/semester confusion is gone (NEU fully switched to semesters), but the culture still moves quickly.

NUworks (NUworks.northeastern.edu) is your co-op platform. Even if co-op feels far away as a freshman, set up your profile in the first month. Upload your resume and a clean headshot. Employers start reviewing profiles earlier than you'd think.

**Campus Geography**
The main academic campus runs along Huntington Avenue, from roughly Forsyth St to the MFA. Key landmarks:
- Snell Library: the main library. Has 24-hour rooms. Reserve study rooms online at least a day in advance during midterms/finals.
- Curry Student Center: the main student hub. Food, clubs, printing.
- Ell Hall / Karman Engineering Center: main engineering buildings.
- International Village: best dining, also residential.

The ISEC building (Interdisciplinary Science and Engineering Complex) is the newest academic building — modern, open study spaces, great for collaborative work.

**The Co-op Culture**
Northeastern has a co-op culture that permeates everything. Students who did competitive co-ops are well-regarded, and the connections you make during co-op often matter more for your career than your GPA. Start thinking about it early even if it's not required until sophomore/junior year.

**Transit**
The Orange Line stop "Ruggles" is the most useful for Northeastern — it's literally on campus. The Green Line E branch stops at "Northeastern" station, also very close. The #39 bus runs along Huntington Ave and is frequently the fastest way to get downtown during rush hour when the E train is slow.

**Money Stuff**
- Northeastern's tuition and housing costs are high. Use the food pantry (located in Curry Student Center) if you're food insecure — it's anonymous and free. Many students use it.
- The Health and Counseling Center (360 Huntington) offers free mental health sessions. Book early in the semester; wait times increase around midterms.
- MBTA Student Semester Pass: buy it through the Northeastern Commuter Student Services office. Significantly cheaper than paying per ride for the full semester.

**The "NEU Grind" Culture**
Northeastern students are generally very career-focused. This is a strength — the co-op program puts you ahead — but it can also create anxiety around comparison. Internship offers and co-op placements are frequently discussed. Be aware of this and find the balance that works for you.
