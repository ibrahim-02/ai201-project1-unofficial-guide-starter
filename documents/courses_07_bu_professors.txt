**Computer Science**

*Prof. Azer Bestavros* — Distributed systems and networks. Legendary at BU. His lectures are dense but packed with real insight. He's been at BU for decades and has a genuine passion for teaching. Exams are hard but fair. Highly recommend if you're serious about systems.

*Prof. Ioannis Paschalidis* — Systems Engineering. Very clear explanations, structured course, fair grading. Goes out of his way during office hours. One of the best office hour experiences I've had — he actually engages with your confusion rather than just pointing to the textbook.

*Prof. Mark Crovella* — Networking and data analysis. Interesting research background (pioneered web traffic analysis). His courses are challenging but his lectures are engaging. The assignments are real-world motivated.

**Mathematics**

*Prof. Kevin Houston* — Calculus and Linear Algebra. Students consistently rate him as one of the best math teachers at BU. He explains concepts intuitively before going rigorous, which makes a big difference in MA 123/124. His notes are excellent and he posts them online.

*Prof. Ryan Goh* — Differential Equations. Patient, approachable, clear. Office hours are not intimidating. Good choice if you're worried about MA 226.

**Engineering (ENG/EC)**

*Prof. David Castañon* — Control Systems. Very organized, clear rubrics, fair exams. His course has a steep learning curve but he scaffolds it well. Responds to email quickly.

**General Tips for Choosing Professors**
- Check RateMyProfessors (ratemyprofessors.com) and search "Boston University" — filter by department. Read recent reviews (past 2 years) since professors can change.
- Post here before registering — someone will have direct experience.
- If a course has multiple sections, the professor matters more than the meeting time in most cases. Switch sections if you need to.
- Office hours are genuinely different at BU vs. high school. Professors here often know individual students by name. Show up.
