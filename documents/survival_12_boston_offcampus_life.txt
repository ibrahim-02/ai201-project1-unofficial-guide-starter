Lived off-campus for three years near both campuses. Here's what actually matters.

**Best Neighborhoods for Students**

*Allston/Brighton (primarily BU)*
The quintessential BU off-campus neighborhood. High density of college students, lots of bars and restaurants on Harvard Ave and Brighton Ave, and generally the cheapest rents within walking distance of BU's West Campus. Downside: loud on weekends (every weekend), and "Allston Christmas" (September 1st, when everyone moves out and leaves furniture on the street) is chaotic. The 57 bus and several Green Line stops connect to campus.

*Mission Hill (primarily NEU)*
20 minutes walk to NEU or one Orange Line stop. Quieter than Allston, a bit more community-feeling. The Mission Hill Food Co-op on Terrace St has good produce. Roxbury Crossing and Jackson Square stations (Orange Line) are nearby.

*Fenway/Kenmore*
The area between BU and NEU. More expensive, but extremely convenient for both schools. Walking distance to Fenway Park (worth it for one game at least), Whole Foods on Westland Ave, and many restaurants on Boylston St. Good if you have roommates to split rent.

*Jamaica Plain (JP)*
Farther from campus (Orange Line from Northeastern or Green Street/Stony Brook stops) but significantly cheaper and arguably the most livable neighborhood in Boston. Great food scene, strong local community, parks. Many grad students live here. Worth considering if you have reliable transit access and want more space for your money.

**Grocery Shopping**
- Trader Joe's: Coolidge Corner (C Line) and one near Fenway. Best value for staples.
- Market Basket: Cheapest grocery option in the area, best in Cambridge or Chelsea if you're willing to travel. Worth it for a big weekly shop.
- Star Market: On Comm Ave near BU; convenient but expensive.
- Whole Foods: Westland Ave (Fenway area). Good quality, premium prices; use it for specialty items.
- H Mart (Korean grocery): South Bay / online delivery. Excellent for Asian groceries.

**Health and Wellness**
- BU students: The Student Health Services at 881 Comm Ave handles general medical needs. Use it for free rather than going to urgent care for minor issues. Mental health: SHS also offers counseling — there are wait times, so book early in the semester.
- NEU students: The Health and Counseling Center at 360 Huntington Ave. Same advice: book early, especially before midterms.
- Both campuses: The Rec centers (FitRec at BU, SquashBusters/Cabot Center at NEU) are included with tuition and genuinely good. Use them.

**Staying Sane**
Boston can feel gray and cold from November through March. Some survival tactics:
- The Arnold Arboretum in JP is free and beautiful in spring — worth the T ride.
- The Museum of Fine Arts (Huntington Ave) has free student admission with school ID for NEU students; BU students get discounted access.
- The MBTA commuter rail can get you out of the city for under $20 round trip — Salem, Gloucester, and Plymouth are all accessible weekend trips.
