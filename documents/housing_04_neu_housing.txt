Here's everything I wish I'd known about NEU housing before I arrived.

**Freshman Dorms**

*White Hall* — Best freshman dorm in my opinion. Newer building, suite-style rooms, air conditioning (not every dorm has this!), and centrally located near Snell Library and the main quad. The social atmosphere is great without being chaotic like some of the bigger dorms.

*Speare Hall* — Also solid. Traditional dorm with hall bathrooms. Popular with engineers because it's close to Snell Engineering. A bit older but the rooms are a decent size.

*East Village (EV)* — The newest residential complex on campus. Suite-style living, modern amenities, and close to International Village dining. The downside is it feels a bit removed from the rest of campus socially — it's at the edge of the main area.

*Stetson Hall* — The giant freshman dorm. Same pros/cons as Warren Towers at BU: great for meeting people, bad if you value quiet. The sheer number of residents means there's always something happening, for better or worse.

**The Co-op Housing Situation**
This is unique to Northeastern. When you go on co-op (typically starting your second year, 6-month work placement), you need to find your own housing unless your co-op is local. If you have a Boston co-op, many students sublet their campus housing or move off campus temporarily. If your co-op is in another city (NYC, SF, Seattle — common for CS students), you need to find short-term housing in that city. Furnished Finder and Airbnb monthly rates are popular for this.

**Off-Campus: The Mission Hill / Roxbury Crossing Area**
Many NEU upperclassmen live in Mission Hill, which is a 10-15 minute walk or one Orange Line stop from campus. Rent is noticeably cheaper than the Fenway/Huntington Ave immediate area: expect $1,200–$1,700/month per room. The Mission Hill Facebook groups and SpareRoom are the go-to resources for finding roommates. Safety is generally fine on the main streets, though walking alone late at night in less populated blocks warrants the same caution you'd apply anywhere in a city.

**Practical tips**
- NEU's housing lottery is competitive. Get your application in early and have a roommate agreement ready.
- The housing office (housing.northeastern.edu) has a roommate-matching tool — use it.
- Subletting is common but read your lease carefully. Some buildings allow it, many don't.
- Utilities in Boston: gas heat can be $150–$250/month in winter. Budget for it.
